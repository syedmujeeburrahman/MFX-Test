1. Executive Summary
Purpose
This Business Requirements Document (BRD) defines the operational, functional, and
technical requirements for implementing an integrated Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)
system for an indoor tennis facility using the Odoo platform. The objective of this project is
to replace multiple disconnected software applications and manual business processes with
a single, fully integrated solution that manages all major aspects of operations at Fox Cities
Racquet Club (FCRC).
The completed system will serve as the central operating platform for the organization,
providing a single source of truth for customer information, court scheduling, financial
transactions, inventory, employee management, and operational reporting. Every
department within the club will utilize the same database and business processes,
eliminating duplicate data entry, reducing administrative workload, improving reporting
accuracy, and enhancing the overall member experience.
Business Vision
Fox Cities Racquet Club seeks to operate with the efficiency and professionalism of a
modern service organization while maintaining the personalized experience expected by its
members. Technology should simplify daily operations rather than create additional
administrative work.
The new ERP system will enable staff to perform all customer-facing and administrative
functions from a unified interface. Whether a customer is booking a court, registering for an
activity, purchasing merchandise, paying membership dues, or enrolling in a league, every
transaction should be recorded within a single integrated system.

performance through comprehensive dashboards, financial reporting, and operational
analytics.
Project Objectives
The implementation will pursue the following primary objectives:
•

Establish a single centralized database for all customers, members, employees,
vendors, products, and financial information.

•

Provide electronic court scheduling for members, staff, tennis professionals, and
league administrators.

•

Automate recurring membership billing and customer invoicing.

•

Integrate retail sales, inventory management, purchasing, and accounting into one
workflow.

•

Eliminate duplicate data entry across departments.

•

Improve financial reporting through integrated accounting and real-time
transaction posting.

•

Streamline payroll processing and employee management.

•

Improve customer service by providing staff with immediate access to necessary
customer information.

•

Increase operational efficiency through workflow automation and standardized
business processes.

•

Support future organizational needs without requiring replacement of core software
systems.

Guiding Principles
The implementation should adhere to the following guiding principles throughout the
project:
Single Source of Truth
Customer information, financial records, inventory quantities, and scheduling
information shall exist only once within the system. Duplicate records and
redundant data entry should be eliminated wherever possible.
Integration Before Customization
Whenever practical, standard Odoo functionality should be configured before
custom development is considered. Customizations should be reserved for
operational requirements that cannot reasonably be achieved through configuration
or existing modules.
Operational Simplicity
The system should simplify daily workflows for employees. Common tasks
performed by front desk staff, instructors, and managers should require minimal
clicks and minimal training.
Financial Accuracy

4

Every financial transaction should flow automatically into the accounting system
without requiring duplicate entry or manual reconciliation. Sales, memberships,
invoices, payroll, inventory purchases, and vendor payments should generate
appropriate accounting entries automatically.
Scalability
The solution should support future expansion, including additional courts (e.g.,
tennis courts that double as pickleball courts), employees, programs, memberships
types, and retail offerings without requiring fundamental redesign. The possibility
of online court reservation functionality should also be taken into account.
Security
Users should have access only to the information necessary to perform their
responsibilities. Financial information, payroll records, and sensitive customer data
shall be protected through role-based permissions (e.g., Front Desk staff will need
access to Scheduling and Point of Sale, but not Accounting module).
Data Integrity
Business rules should prevent invalid transactions, scheduling conflicts, duplicate
customer records, inaccurate inventory counts, and incomplete financial
transactions whenever possible.
Definition of Project Success
The project will be considered successful when the new ERP system enables employees to
conduct daily operations entirely within the integrated platform while improving efficiency,
reducing administrative effort, and increasing the accuracy of operational and financial
information.
Upon completion, management should have real-time visibility into every significant aspect
of club operations, including court utilization, memberships, customer activity, retail sales,
inventory levels, financial performance, and overall business health.
The completed system should serve as the organization's primary operational platform for
the next decade and provide a flexible foundation for continued growth, process
improvement, and future technology integration.

3. Current Challenges
Overview
Fox Cities Racquet Club currently utilizes a combination of independent software
applications, manual processes, spreadsheets, and paper-based workflows to manage daily
operations. While these systems have supported the organization over time, they require
significant manual intervention, duplicate data entry, and reconciliation across
departments.
These disconnected processes increase administrative workload, reduce operational
efficiency, and limit management's ability to make timely, data-driven decisions. The
purpose of this ERP implementation is to eliminate these inefficiencies by creating a fully
integrated business management platform.

11

The following business challenges (BC) have been identified as primary drivers for this
project.

BC-001: Disconnected Software Systems
The club currently relies on multiple software applications and manual processes to
perform daily operations. Customer information, scheduling, accounting, point of sale,
inventory, payroll, and reporting often exist in separate systems that do not share data
automatically.
As a result, staff frequently enter the same information multiple times, increasing both labor
costs and the likelihood of data inconsistencies.
Business Impact
•

Duplicate data entry.

•

Increased administrative workload.

•

Greater opportunity for human error.

•

Delayed access to accurate information.

•

Higher training requirements for employees.

Desired Outcome
A single integrated ERP platform where information entered once is immediately available
throughout the organization.

BC-002: Duplicate/Inaccessible Customer Data
Customer information is often maintained in multiple locations throughout the organization
or in many cases is unavailable to employees needing access. Changes to addresses, phone
numbers, email addresses, family relationships, or membership status are often unavailable
to staff members due to disparate methods of collection/storage or the lack of a shared
electronic platform.
This increases the likelihood of conflicting information and reduces confidence in customer
records.
Business Impact
•

Inconsistent customer information.

•

Incorrect billing information (membership class, etc).

•

Communication errors.

•

Reduced customer service quality.

Desired Outcome
One centralized customer record serving as the authoritative source of customer
information for every department.

BC-003: Manual Financial Reconciliation
Financial transactions generated by different systems require manual reconciliation before
financial statements can be prepared.
Retail sales, membership revenue, lessons, leagues, inventory purchases, and payroll
frequently require additional manual journal entries or spreadsheet manipulation.
Business Impact
•

Time-consuming daily reports and month-end closing.

•

Increased data entry and accounting workload.

•

Greater potential for financial errors.

•

Delayed customer billing and financial reporting.

Desired Outcome
Automatic financial posting from operational transactions into the general ledger with
minimal manual intervention.

BC-004: Limited Reporting Capabilities
Because operational data exists in multiple systems, management reporting often requires
manual compilation from several sources.
Generating reports on membership trends, court utilization, tennis revenue streams, retail
performance, or customer activity can require exporting data into spreadsheets for analysis.
Business Impact
•

Delayed management reporting.

•

Limited operational visibility.

As customer information changes, communication lists may not remain synchronized,
increasing the likelihood that members receive outdated, duplicate, or inconsistent
information. Staff must also spend considerable time determining which customers should
receive specific communications.
Business Impact
•

Duplicate or inconsistent customer communications.

•

Time-consuming maintenance of email and contact lists.

BP-001: Member Registration and Account Creation
Purpose
To establish a new customer account, create membership records when applicable, collect
required documentation, and enable the customer to participate in club activities.
21

Primary Participants
•

Prospective Member

•

Front Desk Staff

•

General Manager

Current Process
A prospective member visits the club or calls by telephone with a membership inquiry.
Front desk staff manually collect customer information including contact information,
household members, membership type, and other required details.
Membership applications are completed using paper forms or separate electronic
documents. Liability waivers are currently NOT collected.
Customer information is entered into Sage accounting software when their account is
created in our system. Sage serves as the primary source of information, with paper
membership application forms sometimes available as back-up.
Membership billing information is limited to the contact information (name, address, phone
number) provided on the membership application. Payment information is NOT collected.
Once complete, the customer becomes eligible to reserve courts, register for programs, and
make purchases, most often through an in-house charge account that is billed early the
following month.
Current Challenges
•

Customer information is often unavailable to desk staff.

•

Paper forms require manual filing.

•

Customer records may become duplicated.

•

Historical documents are stored separately from customer records.

Opportunities for Improvement
The ERP should create a single customer profile containing all personal information,
membership details, family relationships, billing information, signed documents,
communication history, and activity records.
Membership activation, billing setup, and customer communications should occur
automatically through standardized workflows. Contact information (but not payment
information if collected) should be available to desk staff.

22

BP-002: Membership Billing and Account Maintenance
Purpose
To maintain active memberships, process recurring billing, manage account changes, and
ensure customers remain in good standing.
Primary Participants
•

Front Desk Staff

•

Accounting Staff

•

General Manager

Current Process
Membership billing is processed according to annual billing cycles, with the membership
year running from September 1 through the following August. Members joining during that
12-month period are billed on a pro-rated basis (e.g., a member joining in January would be
charged for an 8-month membership).
Memberships are automatically renewed for the following membership year at the end of
July via hand-written invoices Endnote i. Members who wish to cancel then have the cost of the
next year’s membership credited back to their account (again, via a hand-written invoice).
Customer statements are printed and hand delivered or mailed each month.
Payments received through cash, check, or credit card are manually applied to customer
accounts.
Staff investigate billing discrepancies as they arise.
Current Challenges
•

Manual review of recurring billing.

•

Printed monthly statements.

•

Manual payment application.

•

Delayed account updates.

•

Administrative effort associated with billing corrections.

Opportunities for Improvement
Recurring memberships should invoice automatically.
Electronic statements should replace printed statements.

Online payment options should automatically update customer accounts.
Membership status should update in real time.

5. Scope
Purpose
This section defines the scope of the ERP implementation project and establishes the
guiding principles that will govern solution design, software configuration, custom
development, and future system maintenance.
The objective of this implementation is to deploy an integrated business management
solution that supports the operational needs of the indoor tennis facility while minimizing
implementation cost, project complexity, and long-term maintenance requirements.

Project Philosophy
The organization has selected Odoo because of its comprehensive suite of integrated
business applications and its ability to support multiple operational functions from a single
platform.
The preferred implementation approach is to maximize the use of standard Odoo
functionality through configuration before considering custom software development.
Customizations should only be recommended when a documented business requirement
cannot be reasonably satisfied through:
•

Standard Odoo applications

•

Native Odoo configuration options

•

Approved Odoo Studio customizations

•

Existing supported Odoo modules

The organization recognizes that excessive customization increases implementation cost,
testing effort, future maintenance requirements, software upgrade complexity, and overall
project risk.
Accordingly, every proposed customization should demonstrate clear business value that
outweighs these additional costs.

Configuration-First Principle
38

The implementation team shall follow the principles below throughout the project:
Configure Before Customizing
Whenever practical, business processes should be adapted to standard Odoo functionality
rather than modifying core application behavior.
Minimize Custom Code
Custom development should be limited to requirements that provide significant operational
value and cannot be achieved through standard configuration.
Preserve Upgradeability
Solution design should prioritize compatibility with future Odoo upgrades.
Custom modules should avoid modifying core Odoo code whenever possible.
Favor Standard Business Practices
Where appropriate, existing business processes may be refined or standardized to align
with proven ERP practices rather than reproducing inefficient legacy workflows.
Reduce Total Cost of Ownership
Implementation decisions should consider not only initial development cost but also longterm maintenance, support, training, documentation, and future enhancements.

9. Data Migration
Purpose
The successful implementation of the ERP system depends upon the accurate migration of
business data from existing systems into Odoo. The purpose of this section is to define the
organization's expectations regarding data conversion, validation, reconciliation, and
historical data retention.
The primary objective of the data migration process is to provide a complete, accurate, and
reliable foundation for daily operations while minimizing implementation risk and
maintaining financial continuity.
The implementation partner shall develop a detailed Data Migration Plan based upon these
business requirements and identify any assumptions, risks, or limitations prior to beginning
the migration process.
64

Migration Philosophy
The organization recognizes that not all historical data provides equal business value.
Rather than migrating every historical transaction from legacy systems, the implementation
should prioritize:
•

Accurate master data.

•

Complete active operational records.

•

Financial continuity.

•

Historical reporting capability.

•

Minimized implementation complexity.

•

Reduced project risk.

Where practical, historical detail that is not required for ongoing operations may remain
accessible within the legacy Sage 50 system or through archived reports.
